Introduction
How to Use This Guide
A reference for operations administrators who manage the Anna.I service configuration.

This guide is designed for non-technical administrators who are responsible for managing the Configuration section of the Anna.I operations dashboard. The Configuration page is the central control panel where service categories, job types, pricing rules, and autonomy settings are defined and maintained. Every change made here directly affects how jobs are priced, what information customers provide when booking, and how the autonomous dispatch system behaves across the platform.

The document is organized by service category. Within each category, you will find every job type with a detailed breakdown of four key configuration areas: Basic information (name, base price, active status), Pricing Rules (how the final price is calculated including multipliers and surcharges), Required Fields (the questions customers must answer when booking), and Add-ons (optional extras they can purchase). Each section includes tables showing exact field names, values, and their meanings in plain language.

Four Configuration Tabs

The dashboard Configuration page has four tabs that work together. The Pricing tab sets category-level base prices and the platform commission rate. The Categories tab controls which categories are visible to households. The Job Types tab lets you toggle individual services on or off and edit their detailed settings. The Autonomy Thresholds tab defines how many completed job cycles a service provider needs before the system grants higher levels of automated dispatch. Understanding how these tabs interact is essential for making configuration changes correctly and avoiding unintended side effects.

All monetary values in this guide are displayed in Singapore Dollars (SGD, $). In the database, prices are stored in cents (e.g., $80 is stored as 8000). When editing values in the dashboard, you will typically work with dollar amounts in the user interface. The autonomy thresholds section explains the five-level progression system that controls how much automation each vendor receives based on their track record and completed job history on the platform.

How Pricing Works
Understanding the price calculation model used across all job types on the platform.

Every job type in Anna.I uses one of four pricing models to calculate the final amount charged to a household. The pricing model determines whether the base price is charged once per booking, multiplied by a unit count, or scaled by a field value. Understanding these models is critical because they affect how the quote is presented to the customer and how the vendor payout is computed after the job is completed and confirmed.

The Four Pricing Types
TypeHow It WorksExample
flatA single base price charged per booking. Multipliers and surcharges may still apply on top of the base.Deep Cleaning: $300 per session, adjusted by floor area multiplier.
per_unitBase price multiplied by the number of units specified in a required field (unitField).Standard Aircon Service: $50 per unit x number of units selected.
per_itemBase price multiplied by item count. Similar to per_unit but for discrete items.Dry Cleaning: $10 per item x number of items entered.
per_roomBase price multiplied by room or wall count. Used for painting and space-based services.Room Painting: $320 per room x number of rooms to paint.
Multipliers and Surcharges

Most job types include a multiplierField in their pricing rules. This references a required field whose selected option carries a numeric multiplier value. The areaMultiplier flag tells the system to automatically apply a floor-area-based multiplier from the required fields, commonly used in cleaning services. Surcharges are fixed additional amounts triggered by specific field selections, such as a ceiling cassette surcharge for aircon work or an after-hours premium. Surcharges can be flat amounts or per-unit amounts depending on the job type.

Final Price = basePrice x multiplier x (areaMultiplier or 1.0) + surcharges + add-ons
Commission and Vendor Payout
Pricing Formula

The platform retains a commission on every job. The default commission rate is 10%. The vendor receives the remainder. For example, a $100 job yields a $90 vendor payout. The Blended Job Value metric shown on the Pricing tab is the average base price across all active categories, currently benchmarked at SGD $68.00. This figure helps the operations team monitor overall pricing health and identify categories that may need adjustment to remain competitive in the Singapore market.

Vendor Payout = Total Job Price x (1 - commission_rate)
Current Commission Rate = 10% (0.10)

Understanding Autonomy Thresholds
How the five-level automation ladder controls vendor dispatch behaviour per category.

The autonomy system determines how much automated control the system has over job dispatching for each vendor on a per-category basis. New vendors start at Level 1, where every job requires manual operator intervention. As they complete more jobs successfully, they advance through the levels, gaining increasing independence. The thresholds are configured per category on the Autonomy Thresholds tab of the dashboard, allowing different categories to have different ramp-up speeds based on complexity and risk.

The Five Autonomy Levels
LevelNameBehaviour
L1ManualOperations team dispatches every job. The system provides no automated suggestions. This is the starting level for all new vendors to ensure quality and safety oversight during their initial engagement period.
L2Guided SuggestionsThe system analyses demand and availability, then suggests optimal assignments. An operator reviews and confirms each suggestion. This introduces automation while maintaining human oversight for quality assurance.
L3Auto-DispatchThe system automatically assigns jobs based on matching algorithms. The vendor can cancel, and operators can intervene. This is the standard level for established, reliable vendors on the platform.
L4Predictive Pre-BookingThe system uses demand forecasting to pre-book vendors for anticipated jobs before formal requests arrive. This reduces response times and improves service reliability during peak demand periods.
L5Full AutonomousThe system manages the vendor's entire schedule including pre-booking, dispatch, and rescheduling. Vendors at this level have demonstrated consistent reliability and require minimal human oversight.
Cycles Required Per Level

A "cycle" represents one completed job in that category. To advance from L1 to L2, a vendor must complete the specified number of cycles in that category. The same number applies for each subsequent level advancement. Categories with higher risk or complexity require more cycles before granting autonomy. This graduated approach protects service quality while rewarding consistent performance with greater operational efficiency.

Important Note
Autonomy levels are tracked per vendor, per category. A vendor may be at L4 for Cleaning but only L2 for Electrical. This granular approach ensures that automation is only granted where the vendor has proven competence. Thresholds can be adjusted by operations admins if market conditions or vendor performance data suggest a change is needed.
Introduction
Pricing Tab Configuration
Understanding the category-level pricing controls on the Configuration page.

The Pricing tab on the Configuration page provides category-level controls that work in conjunction with the job type pricing rules. While individual job types define their own base prices and calculation logic, the Pricing tab allows operations administrators to set override base prices at the category level and manage the platform's commission structure. This tab serves as the high-level financial control panel for the entire service marketplace.

Category Base Prices

Each active category has a base price that represents the average expected job value for that category. This value is used primarily for reporting and for calculating the Blended Job Value metric. The actual price charged to customers is determined by the individual job type's pricing rules (base price, multipliers, surcharges), not directly by the category base price. However, the category base price serves as a sanity check and benchmark when setting individual job type prices. If a job type's base price deviates significantly from the category average, it may indicate a configuration error that needs investigation.

Commission Rate
Commission Configuration

The platform commission rate is currently set to 10%. This single rate applies uniformly across all categories and job types. The commission is deducted from the total job price (base price plus all multipliers, surcharges, and add-ons) before the vendor payout is calculated. When adjusting this rate, consider the impact on vendor economics and marketplace competitiveness. Even small changes can significantly affect vendor willingness to accept jobs, especially for lower-value services where the absolute dollar commission is already modest.

Blended Job Value

The Blended Job Value is a computed metric equal to the average base price across all active job types in active categories. As of this guide's publication, the baseline is SGD $68.00. This metric is used internally by the operations team to monitor pricing trends and identify categories that may need adjustment. A declining blended value might indicate that lower-priced job types are being added or that existing prices need upward revision. An increasing blended value could signal that the platform is becoming less accessible to budget-conscious households.

Categories Tab vs. Job Types Tab

The Categories tab controls whether an entire service category is visible to households. Setting a category to Inactive hides all its job types from the booking flow, regardless of individual job type active status. The Job Types tab provides finer-grained control, allowing you to toggle individual services within an active category on or off. Use the Categories tab for broad market decisions (e.g., launching or pausing an entire service line) and the Job Types tab for tactical adjustments (e.g., temporarily disabling a specific service due to vendor shortage).

Common Add-on Patterns
After-Hours / Emergency Surcharges

Available on: Plumbing (all 4 types, $40), Electrical (Power Trip $40, Power Point $40), Handyman (Door Repair $30), Locksmith (Emergency Unlock $40). These surcharges compensate vendors for working outside standard business hours and should only be applied when the customer explicitly requests after-hours service through the Service Time or After-Hours add-on selection.

Express / Same-Day Services

Available on: Laundry (Wash/Fold $10, Ironing $8, Dry Cleaning $5/item, Curtains $20), Appliance Repair (Same-Day $30). Express services require the vendor to prioritize the job above their standard queue. Pricing reflects both the urgency premium and the potential disruption to the vendor's schedule.

Per-Unit Add-ons

Some add-ons scale with the number of units or items. Examples: Aircon Filter Replacement ($15/unit x unitCount), Aircon Anti-Bacterial Wash ($20/unit), Electrical Concealed Wiring ($50/point), Painting Ceiling Paint ($50/room). These ensure proportional pricing when a customer needs the add-on applied across multiple units or areas within a single booking.

Configuration Best Practice
When making configuration changes, always test the pricing calculation with sample values before publishing. Verify that the final price shown to the customer matches your manual calculation using the formula: Base Price x Multiplier + Surcharges + Add-ons. Pay special attention to per_unit and per_item types where the unit count significantly affects the total. Document any changes in the operations changelog for audit purposes.
